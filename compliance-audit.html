The 10-Point AI Compliance Audit
for Medicare Agencies.

A plain-English checklist to run against your current enrollment workflow. Written from inside telesales operations. Each point tells you the question auditors ask, why it matters, what they actually look for, and a quick check to run today.

⎙ Print or save as PDF
POINT 01

Call Recording & Retention

Do you record every enrollment call, and can you produce any one of them on demand for the next 10 years?

CMS requires MA/PDP calls to be recorded and retained for 10 years. If you can't produce a call, you can't defend it. This is the single most common finding in Medicare agency audits.
  • 100% call recording coverage (not just outbound, not just sales)
  • Searchable retrieval by date, agent, beneficiary name, or MBI
  • 10-year retention policy documented in writing
  • Encryption at rest; access logs for recording retrieval
Pick a random enrollment from 18 months ago and try to pull the full call recording in under 2 minutes. If you can't, there's your gap.
POINT 02

Scripted Disclosures

Are the five required disclaimers read verbatim on every call, in the right order, before plan details are discussed?

CMS has specific scripted language that must be delivered word-for-word. "Close enough" wording is not compliant. Agents under pressure skip or paraphrase.
  • Non-exclusive agent / "I do not offer every plan available" disclosure
  • Number of plans and carriers you represent in the beneficiary's area
  • Licensed-agent identification
  • TPMO disclaimer when applicable
  • Permission to contact / do-not-call status check
Pull the last 10 enrollment calls and listen for the first 90 seconds. Count how many hit all five. If it's under 10, your script discipline is leaking.
POINT 03

Scope of Appointment (SOA)

Do you capture a valid SOA before discussing any Medicare Advantage or Part D plan, and can you prove it?

SOA is required before any MA/PDP discussion. The 48-hour pre-appointment rule has exceptions, but the SOA itself does not. No SOA = unauthorized marketing. Full stop.
  • Written, recorded, or electronic SOA on file for every enrollment
  • Scope limited to the product types the beneficiary agreed to discuss
  • Timestamp proving SOA precedes the sales conversation
  • Beneficiary signature or verbal confirmation captured
For your last 20 enrollments, is there an SOA timestamp? Does it predate the recorded sales call? Any gaps are liability.
POINT 04

Agent Training & Certification Logs

Can you prove every agent on your floor is AHIP-certified and carrier-appointed for the plans they're selling, as of today?

Agents who sell outside their appointment produce invalid enrollments and personal liability for the agency. Expired AHIP = instant audit red flag.
  • Annual AHIP certification tracked with expiration dates
  • Carrier-specific product training complete per carrier
  • State license current for every state the agent sells in
  • Automated reminder workflow 60 / 30 / 7 days before expiration
Export your agent roster with cert dates. Count how many AHIP certs expire in the next 60 days. If no one owns that reminder flow, you'll have a gap at AEP.
POINT 05

QA Sampling Methodology

What percentage of calls do you grade, who grades them, and does the scorecard reflect what CMS actually audits?

Random, unweighted QA misses systematic issues. If your QA sampling doesn't mirror what carriers and CMS inspect, you're grading the wrong things.
  • Sampling plan documented (random + risk-based: new agents, high-complaint)
  • Scorecard aligned to the carrier's own agent-oversight rubric
  • Pass/fail thresholds trigger coaching workflows
  • QA trending reports produced monthly
If you can't produce a QA scorecard for a specific agent and a specific month in under 5 minutes, your oversight story is weak.
POINT 06

CMS Marketing Rules Compliance

Does every piece of marketing — scripts, landing pages, lead-gen vendors, social posts — meet current CMS marketing rules?

The 2024 TPMO rule tightened everything. Non-compliant marketing from a downstream vendor still comes back to you. CMS is enforcing aggressively.
  • Pre-approved scripts filed with each carrier
  • TPMO disclaimer on all applicable marketing
  • Lead vendor attestations that leads were obtained compliantly
  • No prohibited superlatives or Medicare-logo misuse
  • 48-hour callback rule honored where it applies
Pull one lead from each lead source. Can you trace exactly how it was generated and whether it meets CMS rules? If not, your vendor pipeline is a liability.
POINT 07

Data Security & PHI Handling

Where is beneficiary PHI stored, who can access it, and do you have signed BAAs with every vendor who touches it?

PHI includes MBI, DOB, address, plan information, and call recordings themselves. HIPAA violations stack per record. Most agencies underestimate their exposure.
  • Encryption at rest and in transit for all PHI
  • Role-based access control (not shared logins)
  • Signed BAAs with CRM, dialer, recording, email, AI vendors
  • Breach notification plan documented
  • Call recordings treated as PHI (because they contain it)
List every vendor that touches a beneficiary record. Which of them have you signed a BAA with? Gaps = unquantified HIPAA liability.
POINT 08

Enrollment Verification Process

Before an application is submitted, do you confirm the beneficiary understands the plan, premium, provider network, and formulary impact?

Rapid disenrollment is a performance metric carriers watch. If beneficiaries don't understand what they bought, they cancel — and that hits your contract.
  • Scripted verification of plan name, premium, effective date
  • Provider lookup completed on the call
  • Formulary check for current medications
  • Verbal confirmation of understanding captured on the recording
What's your 90-day disenrollment rate by agent? Any agent 2x the floor average has a verification problem, not just a volume problem.
POINT 09

Fraud Detection & Reporting

If an agent submits a fraudulent enrollment today, how would you catch it, and what's the reporting workflow?

Carriers now share agent misconduct data. One ghost enrollment can get the agent debarred and the agency flagged across every carrier. Early detection is your defense.
  • Monitoring for pattern anomalies (same address, implausible volume, same signature)
  • Beneficiary callback verification sample
  • Internal fraud reporting mechanism (hotline / form)
  • Carrier notification process when fraud is suspected
  • Documented offboarding workflow for terminated agents
Who at your agency owns fraud monitoring? If the answer is "nobody specifically," you're the backstop by default — and auditors will treat it that way.
POINT 10

Vendor & Technology Audit

Every tool in your stack — CRM, dialer, AI, lead portal, email — is a surface area. Who audits them and how often?

AI and automation accelerate everything, including compliance drift. A new AI feature deployed Tuesday can create a disclosure gap by Thursday if no one is watching.
  • Current inventory of every tool with PHI access
  • Change-management log for script, workflow, or AI model changes
  • AI tools reviewed for PHI exposure in training data
  • Dialer configured for TCPA compliance (no auto-dial to cell without consent)
  • Vendor security questionnaires reviewed annually
List every tool that could speak to a beneficiary or touch their record. When was each last reviewed for compliance drift? Anything over 12 months is overdue.
